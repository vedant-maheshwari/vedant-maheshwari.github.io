Research pillars

one question, three angles

The question underneath all of it: can we build language models — especially small and multilingual ones — whose behavior we can actually verify, rather than just observe? That means studying models as dynamical systems whose internal state can be measured, building capable models that work in low-resource languages instead of assuming English-only, and designing systems that surface their own uncertainty instead of hiding it.

Interpretability & safety

Attention as a dynamical system

Koopman-operator and spectral analysis of LLM attention: SR‑KAE, a sparse Koopman autoencoder that linearizes hidden-state trajectories and flags jailbreak prompts from their eigenvalue instability; EPCOD/DynaRank for entropy-proxy-controlled dynamic rank selection; and CAMEO, a diagnostic protocol and score for evaluation-aware concealment — the tendency of a model to behave differently when it detects it is being tested.

Small & multilingual LMs

Capability that isn't English-only

Currently building a ~500M-parameter domain SLM from scratch at AIISC (data curation through post-training) and separately evaluating 11 teams' domain SLMs at the IAIRO Pramana bootcamp. On the language side: AgriLex and a KCC crop-name dictionary pipeline across 22 Indian languages, unsupervised cross-lingual NMT via denoising autoencoding and back-translation, and bilingual lexicon induction reaching 96.3% F1 in Hindi (100% recall) across Gemma 2, Krutrim, Llama 3.1, Qwen 2.5, Param, and Sarvam.

Reliable generative & medical AI

Models that know what they don't know

MUSAN, a Bayesian-uncertainty sparse-attention network for chest X-ray pneumonia detection with calibrated confidence rather than a bare label. On the generative side, an age-aware sketch-to-photo GAN that disentangles identity, age, and structure into separate, controllable latent factors — the same instinct as the interpretability work, applied to a generative rather than a language model.

A working perspective

early thinking, not a claim
Notes — AI as a convergence, not a subfield

I've been circling an idea about what AI actually is, underneath the engineering. My working view is that it sits at the convergence of five older disciplines: mathematics gives the language to model; computing gives the machinery to implement and simulate; psychology and cognitive science supply hypotheses about intelligence and cognition; nature supplies physical and biological principles worth borrowing; and philosophy supplies the harder questions — what counts as knowledge, what understanding actually is, whether reasoning requires language.

That convergence shows up everywhere once you look for it — memory and surprise-driven retention borrowed from cognitive science, physics-informed learning borrowed from nature, attention and curiosity mechanisms sitting somewhere between psychology and optimization.

Remember→Understand→Apply→Analyze→Evaluate→Create

I've also found it useful to read progress in AI capability loosely against Bloom's Taxonomy — a rough progression from systems that retrieve, to ones that pattern-match, understand context, apply and analyze, and increasingly evaluate and create. It's a simplification, and I'm aware of that; I hold it as a lens worth stress-testing rather than a finished argument. I'd welcome pushback on it from anyone who's thought about intelligence more formally than I have.
